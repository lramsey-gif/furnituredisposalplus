Local know-how

How furniture disposal works in Philadelphia

Philadelphia splits bulky disposal into two tracks. Compactible furniture like couches and bagged mattresses goes out with your regular weekly trash, but you are capped at two large items per collection, and they must be manageable enough for the truck to crush. Appliances and bigger loads go through the city’s separate Residential Bulk Collection Program by appointment, which covers up to four items per visit for buildings of six units or fewer.

The prep matters. Mattresses and box springs must be bagged and sealed in plastic before set-out, refrigerator doors must be removed, and tire rims taken off. Flat-screen TVs, computers, construction debris, and hazardous materials are not accepted curbside and must go to a Sanitation Convenience Center such as the one in Port Richmond. Buildings with seven or more units are not on city collection at all and need a private hauler.

How fast can you pick up furniture in Philadelphia?

Same-day and next-day windows are available across most Philly ZIP codes, from Center City and Fishtown to South Philly, Manayunk, and the Northeast. Availability shows when you enter your item and address.

Do I have to use Philadelphia Streets Department pickup instead?

No. Philadelphia lets you put out only two compactible items, such as a couch or a bagged mattress, with your regular weekly trash, and appliances or larger loads need a separate bulk appointment capped at four items. Buildings with seven or more units use a private hauler. Furniture Disposal Plus carries the piece out from inside the home on a window you choose, with no weekly limit.

What happens to my furniture after pickup in Philadelphia?

Usable pieces go to local donation partners first, including Goodwill, the Salvation Army, and Habitat for Humanity ReStore. What cannot be donated is recycled where facilities exist, and only the remainder is disposed of responsibly. Donation acceptance is decided by the receiving center.

How do I get rid of a mattress in Philadelphia?

Philadelphia requires mattresses and box springs to be bagged and sealed in plastic mattress bags before curbside set-out, and they count against your two-item weekly limit. Furniture Disposal Plus takes the mattress from inside your home, bag and all, so you never handle the Streets Department rule.
