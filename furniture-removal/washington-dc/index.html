Local know-how

How furniture disposal works in Washington DC

DC handles bulky items through the Department of Public Works (DPW), and it is appointment-only. You book a pickup through 311 or 311.dc.gov with an itemized list, get a confirmation number, and set the items at your regular trash point no earlier than 6:30 p.m. the night before and by 6 a.m. on your appointment day. The service is free, but only single-family homes and buildings with three or fewer units qualify, and collection can take up to 14 business days.

There are firm limits. DPW takes no more than seven items per appointment, and refrigerant appliances such as refrigerators and window units need reclamation tagging first. Electronics, tires, and construction or renovation debris are excluded entirely, going to DPW drop-off events or the Fort Totten Transfer Station instead. Setting items out before your appointment window can draw a sanitation ticket, and illegal dumping in DC carries fines from $500 to $5,000.

Furniture removal in Washington DC, common questions

How fast can you pick up furniture in Washington DC?

Same-day and next-day windows are available across most DC ZIP codes, from Capitol Hill and Dupont Circle to Georgetown, Columbia Heights, and Anacostia. Availability shows when you enter your item and address.

Do I have to use DC DPW bulk trash instead?

No. DC DPW collects bulk items free by appointment through 311, but only for single-family homes and buildings with three or fewer units, up to seven items per appointment, and pickup can take up to 14 business days. Buildings with four or more units use a private hauler. Furniture Disposal Plus carries the piece out from inside the home on a window you choose, with no appointment wait.

What happens to my furniture after pickup in DC?

Usable pieces go to local donation partners first, including Goodwill of Greater Washington, the Salvation Army, and Habitat for Humanity ReStore. What cannot be donated is recycled where facilities exist, and only the remainder is disposed of responsibly. Donation acceptance is decided by the receiving center.

Can DC DPW take my electronics or construction debris?

No. DC bulk trash excludes electronics, tires, and construction or renovation debris. Electronics go to DPW drop-off events, and debris must go through a licensed hauler or the Fort Totten Transfer Station. Furniture Disposal Plus can take the furniture and many of the items the city refuses in a single booking.
