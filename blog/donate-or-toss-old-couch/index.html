Donate or toss? What to do with an old couch

A clean, structurally sound couch can often be donated, but many charities turn down sectionals, sleeper sofas, and anything stained or torn because the storage and resale value are not there. If your couch is in good shape, donation is worth trying first. If it is not, or a charity refuses it, the piece can still be removed and recycled.

Ready to clear it out?

When to donate

Donation is the best outcome for a sofa that is clean, free of rips and stains, and still sturdy. Goodwill, the Salvation Army, Habitat ReStore, and local furniture banks all take couches that meet those conditions, and some offer pickup. The catch is that acceptance is not guaranteed, and most charities will not take upholstered pieces with odors, pet damage, or broken frames.

The oversized problem

Sectionals and sleeper sofas are the pieces charities most often refuse, even in good condition, because they are hard to store, hard to move, and slow to resell. A pull-out sleeper also hides a heavy steel frame that makes it a two-person carry. If you have been turned down for one of these, you are not alone, and it does not mean the couch has to go to a landfill.

When to remove and recycle

If the couch is worn out or a charity has passed on it, removal is the practical route. A booked Furniture Disposal Plus pickup takes the couch from wherever it sits, sectionals and sleepers included, and routes usable pieces to donation first while recycling the rest where facilities exist.
