Is it cheaper to remove furniture yourself?

Doing it yourself looks cheaper until you add it up. A rented truck, dump fees, moving pads, and a helper often land between $80 and $200 for a single heavy piece, and that is before your own time and a two-person lift. The DIY route: rent a truck and self-haul

Most furniture will not fit in a car, so the real do-it-yourself path means renting a truck. Local truck rentals in 2026 advertise a low daily base rate, but the sticker is only the start. Mileage runs roughly $0.69 to $0.99 a mile, and fuel, taxes, and an environmental fee stack on top. A furniture dolly or a set of pads is another line item. Add it all up and a short round trip for one or two pieces commonly lands around $95 to $152 for the truck alone, per rental-price databases that track what people actually pay after fees.

Then you still have to get the piece down the stairs, into the truck, and back out at the other end. A sleeper sofa or a loaded dresser is a two-person carry, so the honest DIY budget usually includes a favor owed or a hired helper.

The dump fee nobody quotes

Driving your own furniture to the landfill is not free either. Transfer stations charge by weight or per item, and most have a minimum gate charge whether your load is one couch or a full truck bed. Many facilities add a per-item surcharge on mattresses, box springs, and sofas on top of the base fee. Once you combine the minimum charge, the surcharge, and the drive, a single dump run for a couch or mattress can run $50 to $150 in total, and some facilities only accept certain upholstered items at specific sites.

Free city bulk pickup: cheap, but slow and limited

Many cities offer a bulk or large-item pickup, and where it exists it is often the lowest-cost option. The trade-offs are wait time and rules. Scheduled pickups commonly run two to eight weeks out, item counts are capped, and plenty of programs will not take upholstered furniture, appliances, or anything you have to place off your own property. If you need the space now, or the piece is not on the accepted list, the free route does not help.

So which is cheaper?

DIY can win on raw dollars in two cases: your city offers free bulk pickup and you can wait, or you already own a truck and the piece is a one-person carry. Outside those, once you price the rental, the mileage, the fuel, the dump minimum, and your own afternoon, the gap closes fast, and a booked pickup often costs the same or less with none of the labor.
