How to get rid of an old refrigerator

An old refrigerator cannot go out with the regular trash. Federal law under EPA Section 608 requires the refrigerant, often called Freon, to be recovered by a certified technician before any fridge is scrapped or recycled, and venting it is illegal. That is why most cities either charge a fee, require the doors removed, or refuse a fridge at the curb altogether.

Ready to clear it out?

Why a refrigerator is different from ordinary trash

The refrigerant sealed inside the cooling system is the whole reason a fridge is handled apart from a couch or a mattress. It has to be professionally recovered first, which is a step a landfill or a scrap yard will not do for you. On top of that, many cities require the doors removed to prevent child entrapment, and several ban appliances from the garbage entirely.

Your options, and the catch with each

  • City appliance pickup. Many cities collect a fridge, but often only by appointment, with a fee, and with the refrigerant already recovered and tagged. Waits can run from days to months depending on the city.
  • Retailer haul-away. Some stores take your old fridge when they deliver a new one, but usually only the one being replaced, and only if it is accessible.
  • Utility rebate. Power companies sometimes pick up a working fridge and pay a small rebate, but a dead unit does not qualify.
  • A hauler. A removal service handles the recovery and the heavy lift together, with no appointment or curb set-out.
